Punchline is a time card and invoicing app I built for exactly one user. It works, I use
it daily, and the obvious next step is an MCP server so that logging time happens in a
sentence instead of a form. The backend's already a Worker with a typed data layer, so
exposing it is thin. An afternoon, maybe.

I sketched the tool list in about four minutes:

```
logTime(client, minutes, note)
listUnbilled(client, since)
clientSummary(client)
openInvoices()
createInvoice(client, entryIds)       <-
markPaid(invoiceId, amount, date)     <-
voidInvoice(invoiceId)                <-
```

Then I stared at the last three for a lot longer than that, because something was off
about them and I couldn't say what.

They're writes, sure. So is `logTime`. Only one of them destroys anything, and
`voidInvoice` is honestly the least worrying of the three. They aren't harder to build
either: same handlers, same amount of code. Which is the problem. By every measure I was
actually applying, they looked exactly like the four above them.

## The test is who finds out

Log ninety minutes to the wrong client and it's my problem. I'll catch it, or I won't and
the month comes out slightly wrong, and either way it's a ten second fix. The mistake
starts and ends inside a database I own. Nobody else ever knows it happened.

Issue an invoice to the wrong client and it's *theirs*. It's in their inbox. Depending on
the client it's also in their AP system and forwarded to an accountant who's never heard
of me and now has a question. I can't unsend it. I can apologise, and the apology is the
bit they'll remember.

Those two aren't different in degree. They're different in kind, and what separates them
has nothing to do with reads or writes or how much code sits behind the call:

> When this goes wrong, who finds out, and do they hear it from me?

If the answer's "me, from myself", automate it and stop thinking about it. If it's
"someone else, before I know", that's a different category of operation and it earns a
different level of ceremony.

## Reversibility is the wrong question

The instinct is to ask whether a thing is reversible. Sounds rigorous. It isn't, because
it's quietly asking about the database.

I can delete an invoice from D1 in forty milliseconds. I can't delete it from someone's
inbox, or from their memory of the time I billed them twice. The database isn't where the
consequence lives. Anything that leaves my systems is irreversible in the only sense that
matters, however clean the rollback looks.

Which covers a lot more ground than invoicing. Sending email. Opening a PR against a
shared repo. Deploying. Filing the ticket that pages someone at 3am. All of them are cheap
to undo technically and none of them are undoable socially, and it's the second part that
should decide whether they happen because a sentence parsed correctly.

## "Just add a confirmation step"

The obvious objection, and I think it does less work than it looks like it does.

Confirmation prompts earn their keep when the thing they interrupt is unexpected. In an
agent loop it's never unexpected. I asked for this. I'm mid-flow. The model has just
explained, fluently, what it's about to do and why that's the right call. Clicking confirm
is how I carry on doing the thing I asked for, so I'll click it.

We already know this about ordinary software. Everyone's clicked through a dialog they
didn't read. Bolting a confident, well-argued justification onto that dialog isn't an
improvement, and I'd rather not find out how many times I can be talked round before I
stop reading them altogether.

So the confirmation stays. It's just not the control. The control is that the capability
isn't there.

## What ships

Reads, and `logTime`. That's version one.

Invoicing stays behind the UI, where I open a tab, see the client's name next to the
amount, and click a button that does exactly one thing. Call it fifteen seconds, a handful
of times a month.

That friction isn't a failure to finish the integration. It *is* the integration. Opening
the tab is the control, and fifteen seconds is the cheapest insurance I'll ever buy
against a conversation that starts "hey, quick question about this invoice".

## What it costs me

None of this is free, and the annoying part is that the operations with consequences are
exactly the ones worth automating.

"Invoice everyone for last month" is a twenty minute job I'd love to collapse into a
sentence. It's the most valuable thing an agent could do in this app, and it's the one
thing I'm refusing it. The automation I've kept is the automation that only touches me.
I've drawn the line right around the good bit, and I know it.

"For now" is doing real work in all of this, too. I'll move the line eventually. My guess
is `createInvoice` ends up producing a draft that I still have to release by hand, which
automates the generation and keeps the sending human, and that's probably where it sits
for a long time.

But I wanted the starting position on the record, dated, before I had any experience to
rationalise with. Everyone's publishing what they hooked their agent up to. I haven't seen
many people publish the other list.
