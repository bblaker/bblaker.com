For about a year the cluster booted from SD cards, and for about a year I had a slow
background rate of weird failures: nodes that went read-only, etcd that got unhappy
under write load, and one card that simply stopped existing mid-week.

Every one of those was the storage. I spent an embarrassing amount of that year tuning
things that were not the storage.

## The change

Six NVMe HATs, six 1TB drives, and a `rpi-eeprom-config` change to move NVMe ahead of
SD in the boot order. The tedious part was doing it six times without letting the
cluster drop below quorum.

<Term title="one node at a time">
$ kubectl drain pi-04 --ignore-daemonsets --delete-emptydir-data
node/pi-04 drained
$ ssh pi-04 sudo rpi-eeprom-config --edit   # BOOT_ORDER=0xf416
$ ssh pi-04 sudo reboot
$ kubectl uncordon pi-04
node/pi-04 uncordoned
</Term>

## What actually changed

etcd write latency dropped by roughly an order of magnitude and, more importantly,
stopped having a long tail. The read-only remounts went away entirely. Nothing else
about the cluster changed.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Check your power budget first">
Six NVMe drives is a real addition to the PoE budget, and I got this wrong: two nodes
brown-out rebooted under simultaneous load before I worked out what was happening. Sum
the peak draw, not the idle draw.
</Callout>

The lesson isn't "NVMe is faster", which everyone knows. It's that I spent a year
tuning around a problem I'd have found in a day if I'd measured storage latency before
touching anything else.
