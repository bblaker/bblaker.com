The 350 is in pieces on the bench. That wasn't a failure. It's a machine I know well
enough to strip without anxiety, which is exactly the position building one yourself
puts you in.

## Why it came apart

Two reasons, and only one of them is technical.

The Core One with INDX took over as the workhorse, and once a machine stops being the one
you reach for, its problems stop getting fixed. Rather than let it slowly become a large
object that mostly gathers dust, it seemed better to take it apart while I still remember
how it goes together.

The other reason is simply **space**. A 350 Voron has a substantial footprint, and I'm
short on bench right now. A machine earning its place is not an abstract question when
the thing it's competing with is somewhere to work.

## Rebuild, or repurpose

Genuinely undecided, and that's the interesting part of this project.

**Rebuild as a 2.4.** Bring it back current: updated toolhead, better wiring, and the
handful of things I'd do differently having lived with the first version. A large-format
CoreXY is a genuinely different tool from the Core One rather than a redundant one, and
350mm of build volume solves problems nothing else here can. The cost is that it has to
win its footprint back from a bench I'm already short of.

**Repurpose the frame.** It's a well-built gantry and a lot of good linear motion
hardware. Large-format machines don't have to be printers. The same mechanics serve a
number of other things, and starting from a frame I already trust removes most of the
risk from a more speculative build. Whether that helps the space problem depends entirely
on what it becomes.

**Or neither.** Parting it out is a real option, and pretending otherwise would be
dishonest. Good linear rails and a working set of electronics are worth more in a project
that gets used than in a frame that gets stepped around.

<Callout kind="gotcha" title="The honest constraint">
This is a floor-plan decision more than an engineering one. Every argument above is real,
but none of them decides anything until the machine can justify the desk it would sit on, and right now that desk is the scarcer resource. Writing it down plainly so I don't
later dress up a space decision as a technical one.
</Callout>

The teardown gets documented either way. Whichever direction this goes, the parts
inventory and the notes on what wore out over a few years of running are worth having.
