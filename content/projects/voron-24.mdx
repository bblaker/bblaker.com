The 350 is in pieces on the bench. That wasn't a failure — it's a machine I know well
enough to strip without anxiety, which is exactly the position building one yourself
puts you in.

## Why it came apart

The Core One with INDX took over as the workhorse, and once a machine stops being the
one you reach for, its problems stop getting fixed. Rather than let it slowly become a
large object that mostly gathers dust, it seemed better to take it apart while I still
remember how it goes together.

## Rebuild, or repurpose

Genuinely undecided, and that's the interesting part of this project.

**Rebuild as a 2.4.** Bring it back current — updated toolhead, better wiring, and the
handful of things I'd do differently having lived with the first version. The argument
for this is that a large-format CoreXY is a genuinely different tool from the Core One,
not a redundant one, and 350mm of build volume solves problems nothing else here can.

**Repurpose the frame.** It's a well-built gantry and a lot of good linear motion
hardware. Large-format machines don't have to be printers — the same mechanics serve a
number of other things, and starting from a frame I already trust removes most of the
risk from a more speculative build.

<Callout kind="gotcha" title="The honest constraint">
The deciding factor probably isn't technical. It's whether a second large printer earns
its bench space, and I'm not yet sure it does. Writing that down now so I can check
later whether I talked myself into the answer I wanted.
</Callout>

The teardown gets documented either way. Whichever direction this goes, the parts
inventory and the notes on what wore out over a few years of running are worth having.
