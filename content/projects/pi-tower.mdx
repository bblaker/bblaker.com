Six Raspberry Pi 5s in a rack I designed and printed, running k3s. It started as a
way to stop breaking things at work and turned into the most useful piece of hardware
I own — it runs my monitoring, backups, media, and whatever I'm currently building.

## Why six

Three would have been enough for a control plane and a couple of workloads. Six means
I can drain two nodes at once and still have quorum, which is the difference between
maintenance being a chore and maintenance being a thing I actually do. It also makes
failure interesting rather than terminal: I can pull a node mid-afternoon and watch
what happens.

## What it taught me

Mostly that the boring parts matter more than the clever ones. NVMe boot was worth
more than any tuning I did. Cilium over flannel was worth it, but not for the reasons
I expected. And the PoE budget is a real constraint that I got wrong the first time —
that one is written up in the devlog.
