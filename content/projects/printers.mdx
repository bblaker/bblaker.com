I've been printing since 2017 and have had some version of a printer on the bench ever
since. Machines have come and gone; the habit hasn't. This page is the fleet's home —
repairs, mods, rebuilds and failures land here rather than disappearing into a notes
file.

## Building from scratch, on purpose

Most of these machines I assembled myself, and that's the point rather than a
cost-saving measure. A printer you built is a printer you can fix. When something starts
going wrong at layer 400 there is no substitute for having had every part in your hands
once already — you know what's behind the panel, you know which fastener backs out, and
you know what the machine sounded like when it was right.

It's also the fastest way I know to actually understand a mechanism. Reading about belt
tension teaches you very little. Tensioning a gantry twice, badly, teaches you a lot.

## The current fleet

The **Prusa Core One with the INDX add-on** is the workhorse. Eight nozzles has changed
what I bother to attempt: multi-material stops being an event you plan a whole evening
around and becomes something you just do, which turns out to matter far more than any
individual print it enables.

The **350 × 350 CoreXY** is mid-rebuild. Large-format machines are their own discipline —
everything that's a minor annoyance at 180mm is a structural problem at 350.

A **CocoaPress** is currently being specced, because apparently the fleet now needs a
printer that extrudes chocolate. Different material constraints entirely: temperature
control matters in a way plastic never demanded.

## What's gone

A **Prusa Mini** and a **Voron**, both of which earned their keep as teachers. The Mini
was the machine that made printing routine rather than an event. The Voron was where I
learned that a machine you tuned yourself fails in ways you can predict.

<Callout kind="note">
Nothing here is a review. Every machine on this page was chosen for a reason and has
annoyed me in some specific way since — that's the part worth writing down, and it never
survives into a spec sheet.
</Callout>
